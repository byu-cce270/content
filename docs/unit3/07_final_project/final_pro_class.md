# In Class: Final Project Kickoff

There is no exercise today. Sit with your team. We will go over what the final project asks of you, from the design to the presentation, and answer your questions. The details are on two pages that you will use from now until the end of the semester: [Part 1: Design](final_pro_proposal.md) and [Part 2: Final Project](final_pro_prompt.md).

## The Project

Your team will design, build, validate, and present an app that solves a real problem, using an AI coding agent. We recommend a problem from your major, such as civil engineering, construction management, or facilities and property management, but you are free to be creative. You may build either a web app or a desktop app. You will follow the same process you used in the web app and desktop app homework: write a spec, have the agent review it, build one feature at a time, and validate as you go. The difference is that the problem is bigger and your whole team works in one shared GitHub repository.

## The Three Stages

| Stage | What your team does | What you turn in | Points |
|:--|:--|:--|:--:|
| [Design](final_pro_proposal.md) | Choose a problem, set up the team repository, write the spec, have the agent review it, and plan the validation | `spec.md` and the repository's GitHub address | 30 |
| [Build](final_pro_prompt.md#building-the-app) | Build the app one feature at a time, validate it, and document it | A `.zip` of the project folder | 90 |
| [Presentation](final_pro_prompt.md#presentation) | Demonstrate the app live and walk through how you validated it | Nothing extra, but sign up for a time | 10 |

The build and the presentation are graded together as the 100-point final project.

## Things to Know Before You Start

- **Choose the problem carefully.** It is the hardest part of the project. Read [Choosing a Problem](final_pro_proposal.md#step-1-choose-a-problem), and get a TA's or the instructor's approval for your problem before you write the spec.
- **Design before you build.** Your spec asks for more than the homework specs did: who the users are, what each screen shows, the agent's design review and what you decided about it, and a validation plan with at least three hand-worked cases, all before any code.
- **Teamwork is graded.** Every team member enters their own prompts and commits from their own GitHub account throughout the project, and every team member turns in their own agent chats. Graders check the commit history and the prompt log. See [How Teamwork Is Graded](final_pro_prompt.md#how-teamwork-is-graded).
- **Tell us early.** If you are having trouble working with a team member, tell your professor and the head TA as soon as possible, while there is still time to fix it.
- **Everything is due before presentations start.** The whole project is due before the first presentation in your section, even if your team presents later.
- **Everyone presents.** Every team member must be at your presentation, and any of you may be asked to explain any part of the code.
